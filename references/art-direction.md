# Art direction with presence

## Find the visual idea

Start from what the user is trying to understand. Give the dashboard an organizing metaphor that makes that task memorable. Choose a physical or spatial vocabulary specific enough to guide geometry, lighting, composition, and motion.

| Product question | Visual world | Signature interaction | Evidence that stays explicit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What should I learn next? | Terraced atlas with a selected route and a raised current checkpoint | Selecting a chapter brings its layer forward and reveals prerequisites beside it | Attempts, verified mastery, due reviews, and prerequisites remain separate |
| Where is the system failing? | Precision observatory with a sectional service map | Selecting a service separates its dependencies into a readable inspection view | Real status, timestamps, latency units, incident severity |
| How is the business performing? | Editorial desk with layered comparison sheets | A period switch aligns the two comparison sheets and updates the flat chart | Currency, period, baseline, actual versus forecast |
| What deserves attention today? | Sculpted calendar or paper studio | Selecting a day reveals a bounded stack of tasks beside the timeline | Actual commitments, capacity assumptions, unscheduled work |
| How is this physical system operating? | Exploded product model or geographic scene | Select a part or location; the camera settles while its details open in DOM | Exact measurements, scale, orientation, accessible 2D lookup |

Treat the metaphor as composition, not a fictional simulation. Do not invent plant growth, earned trophies, weather, topology, or status. Label an abstract decorative sculpture as decorative through its semantics; bind data only when the mapping is explicit.

## Compose three candidates, choose one

Briefly consider three silhouettes: a strong left/right split, an anchored central scene with a detail rail, and an editorial stack with one panoramic chart. Choose according to data density, primary action, and device. For a dense operations console, the large chart may be the focal object; for a learning tracker, it may be the next checkpoint.

Use a dominant region, a supporting comparison, and an inspection area. Vary section sizes deliberately. Avoid enclosing every heading, KPI, and sentence in a separate rounded card. Keep the first viewport useful: status, primary evidence, and next action must be visible without scrolling past decoration.

## Material recipes

Use two or three material roles per theme. Define a consistent light source, surface thickness, contact shadow, edge treatment, and highlight behavior. Keep data surfaces opaque enough for predictable contrast.

- **Canopy / terraced landscape:** warm mineral ground, matte cream working surfaces, moss edges, carved contour layers. Use a leaflike cut or topographic seam sparingly. A selected chapter can appear as the nearest terrace. Let motion feel weighted and soft, then settle completely.
- **Form / paper sculpture:** porcelain canvas, crisp folded planes, one cobalt or vermilion accent, subtle colored edge thickness. Repeat a diagonal or an offset seam through the focal object and selected tab. Move related planes along one clear axis.
- **Signal / luminous instrument:** blue-black enamel, smoked opaque plates, fine etched rules, luminous selection edges, pale measurement labels. Use a short response to selection or real incoming events. Keep glows local; keep text sharp.
- **Optional Observatory:** ink and chalk, orbital construction lines, restrained brass accents, a purposeful inspection lens. Use only when the relationship is spatial or a focused detail view is useful; circular layouts must not obscure comparison.

Add small surface details with CSS/SVG when they fit: bevels, inset edges, contour strokes, restrained grain. Use image generation for art assets only when a real bitmap materially improves the result. Never rasterize live labels or charts into decorative imagery.

## Design one memorable interaction

Choose one effect that explains the task:

- Selecting a roadmap node raises its visual layer while the adjacent detail panel updates immediately.
- A slider moves a tangible marker and a linked projection, with actual values and assumptions labeled separately.
- A filter carries a stable selection into the next view while unrelated marks recede.
- An earned milestone resolves a contour or illuminates a route once, based on persisted completion.
- Expanding a service reveals dependency layers; closing restores the original camera and focus.

Use this focal grammar for major changes; use brief presses, selection edges, and state highlights for ordinary controls. An interface should not require the user to wait for choreography.

## Make the refinement visible

After rendering, improve the weakest of these deliberately:

1. **Silhouette:** does the composition remain recognizable as a small screenshot?
2. **Hierarchy:** can the user name the main result and next action in five seconds?
3. **Material:** do light, shadows, edge thickness, and surface colors agree?
4. **Typography:** do headings have character while numbers and controls remain crisp?
5. **Interaction:** does the signature explain a relationship and settle promptly?
6. **Continuity:** does it remain the same product on mobile, in Flat, and with motion Off?

Replace ornamental clutter with a stronger focal object or clearer grouping. Avoid branding every theme with the same floating glass cards, purple gradients, and orbiting particles. Keep the finished scene satisfying at rest.
